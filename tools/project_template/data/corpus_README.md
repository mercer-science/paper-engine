# data/corpus/ — the review's data

For a research paper, `data/` holds what was measured, and every number in the
prose is checked against it. For a review, almost nothing was measured — but
**every sentence is a claim about somebody else's work, and those claims are
exactly as checkable, against exactly the same kind of record.** This folder is
that record.

| file | what it is | who writes it |
|---|---|---|
| `protocol.md` | the scope contract: the question, the inclusion and exclusion criteria, the date window, the queries, the corpus floor | **you**, once. `review.py protocol --init` writes the template and will never write over it again |
| `searches.jsonl` | append-only, one line per query per page: source, terms, filters, hits, new | `review.py search` |
| `screened.csv` | every candidate ever seen, and why each was excluded | `review.py search` / `screen` |
| `papers/<citekey>.md` | one record per included paper, named by citekey | `review.py record` / `adopt` |
| `pdfs/` | what you dropped in, or `pubmed.py pdf` retrieved | you, and the engine |
| `synthesis.md` | the evidence table | GENERATED by `review.py synthesis` |
| `.corpus_state.json` | content hashes: staleness, and the `read_tier` guard | GENERATED |

## The One Field Everything Turns On

Each paper record carries a `read_tier:` line — `title`, `abstract`,
`fulltext`, `pdf` or `figure-digitized` — and a `## Limits of this record`
section the engine writes from it. **No sentence in the review may
characterize a source beyond the tier at which that source was read.**

An abstract licenses the paper's own stated claim and its headline result. It
does not license its n, its conditions, its controls, its limitations, what
its figures show, or that it failed to consider anything — however plainly
true the sentence looks. `review.py tier` is the countable half of that rule;
`attribution-check` is the half that reads.

Measured on a real chemistry topic: of 25 candidates, 24 carry an abstract and
**none** carries a PMC identifier, so the tiered full-text route can read none
of them. A chemistry review built here is an **abstract-tier review** unless
you supply PDFs. That is exactly why the rule exists.

## Three Rules About This Folder

- **`protocol.md` is yours and is never generated over.** The engine proposes
  one from your topic; you correct it.
- **`searches.jsonl` is append-only.** A narrative review a reviewer later asks
  to make systematic cannot reconstruct the searches it did not log, and the
  log is free at the moment the search runs and impossible afterwards.
- **No row is ever deleted from `screened.csv`.** The exclusions are the audit
  — "we looked at it and said no" is the thing a reviewer asks about — and a
  candidate excluded at screening and included later keeps both entries with
  their dates.

## What Does Not Belong Here

Prose. Nothing in this folder is drafted from. `papers/` records what a source
says; `plan/README.md` records what this review argues; and `attribution-check`
is blind to the second while it reads the first.
